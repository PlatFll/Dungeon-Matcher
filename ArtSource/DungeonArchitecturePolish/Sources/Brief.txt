# Dungeon Matcher — Dungeon Architectural Enhancement

## Objective

Use the attached image as the **visual reference for improving the Dungeon zone's side architecture**.

I want the existing Dungeon to feel slightly richer and more atmospheric, similar to the reference, while remaining quieter and more geometric than our Magical Forest zone.

**This is an environmental asset enhancement, NOT a full Dungeon redesign.**

Use PixelLab to generate the necessary individual assets, LibreSprite to clean and finalize them, and Unity to assemble and validate the result.

## 1. Mandatory preparation

Before making changes:

- Read `AGENTS.md`.
- Read `Docs/ArtDirection/Dungeon_Matcher_Art_Direction.txt`.
- Read `Docs/ArtDirection/Golden_Reference_Register.md`.
- Inspect the actual approved Rattlebones sprite, existing Dungeon assets, and the latest playable Dungeon scene.
- Inspect the attached screenshot carefully, particularly the stone architecture to the left and right of the gem board.

Use the latest accepted Dungeon implementation as your foundation.

Work on a new isolated branch named `feature/dungeon-side-architecture-polish`. Preserve all unrelated work.

## 2. What must remain unchanged

**LOCK THESE ELEMENTS:**

- Existing top HUD layout and appearance.
- WAVE plaque, GUIDE button and settings button.
- Existing central dungeon doorway and battle presentation.
- Player portrait frame.
- Character sprites and animations.
- All gem artwork and board-cell graphics.
- Board dimensions, position and gameplay behavior.
- Bottom HUD, resonance panel, potion and bomb controls.
- Health bars, countdown indicators and other gameplay UI.
- Existing game mechanics and other zones.

Do not change their source artwork.

The screenshot is a reference for **environmental architecture only**, not permission to recreate its characters, gems, controls or layout.

## 3. Stage 1 — Generate the side stone pillars

This is the main architectural improvement.

Use PixelLab to create a modular dungeon stone pillar kit based on the attached reference.

### Required components

- Large stone pillar shaft.
- Decorative pillar capital.
- Heavy stone pillar base.
- Horizontal stone ledge and trim connectors.
- Recessed masonry sections.
- Carved skull-relief stone blocks.
- Optional chipped or weathered stone variants.

### Visual requirements

The pillars should:

- Use dark purple and muted violet-gray stone.
- Have chunky rectangular masonry blocks.
- Use restrained top-left highlights.
- Feature deep, clean shadow separations.
- Have strong geometric silhouettes.
- Feel like part of the castle's original construction.

**Important:** The pillars must look like continuous structures, not separate stone sprites pasted vertically on the background.

Generate matching modular parts that can be assembled seamlessly into full-height columns.

Place them on the left and right sides of the board area, following the visual arrangement of the reference.

Keep them outside the playable board.

## 4. Stage 2 — Torches and hanging chains

Use PixelLab to generate or improve the following separate environmental assets.

### Torch assets

Create a chunky wrought-iron dungeon wall torch with:

- A dark iron bracket.
- A readable orange/yellow flame.
- A small ember detail.
- Restrained warm lighting on surrounding stone.

The torch should mount naturally onto the stone pillars.

Reuse the existing torch if it already matches the desired quality; otherwise generate an improved candidate.

### Chain assets

Create:

- Repeatable vertical iron chain segments.
- Chain anchor brackets.
- A few naturally hanging chain-end variations.
- Optional heavier chain connectors for the lower side masonry.

Chains should hang from believable attachment points and follow a continuous path.

**Avoid disconnected floating links, overlapping fragments or excessive chain density.**

Keep the chains dark enough that they remain secondary to the gems and characters.

## 5. Stage 3 — Recessed masonry and skull ornaments

Generate supporting architectural components:

- Skull-carved stone reliefs.
- Dark recessed brick alcoves.
- Decorative masonry ledges.
- Simple stone blocks with restrained damage.
- Small worn-stone transitions.

Integrate the reliefs into the stonework itself.

The skulls should look **carved into the pillars or walls**, not like decorative stickers.

Avoid adding too many. Use them as occasional focal points along the sides.

Do not introduce banners.

## 6. Stage 4 — Unlit candles

Add several small candle groups on appropriate stone ledges.

**All candles must be UNLIT.**

- No candle flames.
- No glow effects.
- No candle illumination.
- No ember particles.

Use small ivory/off-white wax candles of varying heights, with dark wicks and subtle wax-drip details.

Place a few groups around the lower side architecture, as shown in the reference.

The existing wall torches may remain lit.

Ensure candle bases sit correctly on their stone ledges and do not appear to float.

## 7. Stage 5 — PixelLab cleanup and asset construction

For every generated asset:

1. Preserve the raw PixelLab source separately.
2. Open and clean the selected candidate in LibreSprite.
3. Simplify unnecessary shading and texture noise.
4. Normalize material colors against the existing Dungeon art ramps.
5. Repair stray pixels and damaged contours.
6. Ensure binary transparency for isolated props.
7. Fix repeating sections and their seams.
8. Export at the established native source-pixel scale.

Use PixelLab's appropriate available tools for individual objects, architectural modules, and reference-guided edits.

**Do not generate a whole completed Dungeon screen and slice it afterward.**

Do not impose Rattlebones's exact 15-color palette on environment assets. Match his pixel cleanliness and rendering discipline, while using the established dungeon stone, metal, and lighting ramps.

You may use additional PixelLab generations where they materially improve the assets. Record the actual generation usage.

## 8. Stage 6 — Assemble in Unity

Assemble the new architecture in the existing Dungeon scene using separate sprites and repeatable modules.

Build the scene in this order:

1. Existing background masonry.
2. New continuous side pillar structures.
3. Recessed wall panels and carved skull stones.
4. Chains and their mounting hardware.
5. Torch brackets and flames.
6. Stone ledges and unlit candles.

Use the existing Unity presentation systems rather than creating competing UI or environment systems.

Keep the new decorations outside gameplay hit targets, board cells, health bars and important text.

Make sure the architecture works at different supported mobile aspect ratios.

## 9. Validation

Capture real Unity screenshots before and after the changes.

Validate:

- Native-pixel sharpness.
- No blurry or fractional scaling distortion.
- Seamless pillar segments.
- Continuous masonry and chains.
- Correct torch mounting.
- Correct candle placement.
- Candles remain unlit.
- No banners added.
- No overlap with the playable board.
- Consistent colors and shading.
- No changes to locked UI or gameplay assets.

Inspect the entire screen at actual gameplay size, not only enlarged asset crops.

The architecture should make the Dungeon feel more complete, but **it must not become as visually dense as the Magical Forest**.

If a particular decoration looks noisy or unnecessarily prominent, simplify it rather than adding more detail.

## 10. Final deliverables

Provide:

1. Native PNGs and editable LibreSprite sources for all new assets.
2. A reusable modular stone-pillar kit.
3. A working Unity scene with the new architecture.
4. Actual before-and-after Dungeon gameplay screenshots.
5. Close-up previews of the left and right architectural sections.
6. A PixelLab generation usage report.
7. Confirmation that all protected artwork and shared UI remain unchanged.

Proceed through the stages and run validation after each one.

**Stop after the complete architectural enhancement is assembled and verified. Do not merge to main without my explicit approval.**

### Final visual target

A dungeon with **strong geometric stone pillars, recessed masonry, hanging iron chains, carved skull ornaments, lit wall torches, and small unlit candle groups**.

It should feel ancient, mysterious, atmospheric and deliberately constructed.

Keep the architecture visually restrained so the characters and colorful match-3 gems remain the main focus.
